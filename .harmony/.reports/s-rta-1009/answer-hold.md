Sources: research-hold-dj.md ("dj n"), research-hold-vj.md ("vj n"), research-hold-live.md ("live n"), check-hold.md ("re-check"; "re-check own n" = its section WHAT I FOUND MYSELF). The re-check wins wherever it differs. Nothing here was measured or run; every fact about the app is "read, not run" from the s-rta-1005 fact sheet.

## WHAT PEOPLE DO

**DJs.** A button that works only while held is on every deck and controller the research read, but as a fixed habit of one function, not as a switch on every pad: the CUE button, loop roll, Slip and XY-FX are held, and letting go restores what was playing (dj 1 and dj 4 -- NOT re-checked; dj 2, re-check CONFIRMED). Where the DJ chooses, it is one setting for all pads and only while the deck is paused: AlphaTheta "Gate Cue", Denon "Momentary" against "Trigger" (dj 2, dj 3; re-check CONFIRMED). On one Serato pad bank the maker mixes both: pads 1-4 held, pads 5-8 toggle (dj 5; re-check CONFIRMED, an entry-level controller). DJs also ask for the way back, a held effect that stays on, so both directions are wanted (dj 7; re-check CONFIRMED). How sure, by the re-check: high that holding is normal for DJs, medium that it is wanted as a setting.

**People who run visuals or lights for bands.** A held "Flash" or "Bump" button is the standard tool for playing along by hand: grandMA3, ChamSys MagicQ, Avolites, Onyx and Hog all have it, and on MagicQ, Avolites and Onyx it is the button's normal way, with toggle / latch as the option (live 1-5; re-check CONFIRMED). Working lighting people name it for hits, strobes and blinders (live 9, live 11, live 12; re-check CONFIRMED; live 10 CONFIRMED with the hold only inferred). For visuals the same thing is Resolume's "Piano": per clip, per composition and per shortcut, never the starting setting, and Resolume itself says it may be called Momentary or Flash (vj 1, vj 2, live 6, dj 14; re-check CONFIRMED). One named VJ holds an APC40 pad to strobe (vj 5; re-check CONFIRMED, a tutorial, not a gig report), and one Resolume user runs every clip in Piano from a Launchpad (re-check own 3). MadMapper keys are held by default and Visibox has "Gate" per clip (vj 8, vj 11; re-check CONFIRMED). GrandVJ counts only as far as "a held key is its normal way": its latch is one toolbar mode, not a switch per cell (vj 10; re-check WEAKER). Where a desk lacked a held button, people asked for it (live 14; re-check CONFIRMED). How sure, by the re-check: high for lighting, medium for visuals.

**DJ / producers.** Their music tools have it: Ableton Live's "Gate" launch mode per clip ("down starts the clip; up stops the clip"), Traktor's "Gate" against "Latch" per sample with Latch as the starting setting and Gate meant for short hits (dj 9, dj 11; re-check CONFIRMED). For their own visuals the evidence is thin and points the other way: Max Cooper and Madeon let Ableton trigger Resolume, and no interview describes a held pad (dj 15, re-check WEAKER: three interviews cannot speak for all, and "not described" is not "not done"; re-check own 5). The nearest case is a DJ who "punches" strobe hits and blinders on a lighting desk; it is lighting, and the page does not say he holds (re-check own 4). How sure, by the re-check: low.

**What the research did NOT find.** No number or survey of how many performers use a held button, on any side (re-check, WHAT IT DOES NOT SUPPORT 8). No DJ or DJ / producer who holds a pad for visuals, and no proof that they avoid it (re-check 7). No interview with a band's VJ that names it (live NOT FOUND; vj NOT FOUND). Nobody who dislikes it and no program that dropped it (re-check own 1; absence in a search is not proof). Not used here, because the re-check struck or weakened them: "the starting setting is always press" as a general rule (it depends on the family: press for clip launchers, hold for lighting desks and MadMapper keys); the "live secret weapon" blog (dj 10); the djay user as proof that a setting costs surprise (dj 8); Resolume's blackout example as a hold use (live 7: it is the toggle example); the "four flash buttons" advice as visuals evidence (live 16: it is lighting advice).

## IS IT WORTH BUILDING

**Verdict: YES, by his own measure, in the smallest form; confidence medium.** His measure is "We need to only match what Dj or bands are doing". On the band side a held button is the standard tool of the people who play light or picture by hand, and on most desks it is the normal way of the button (live 1-5, 9, 11, 12; re-check CONFIRMED). DJs hold buttons as a matter of course (dj 2, dj 3, dj 5). The one tool built for exactly his job, Resolume, has it per clip and per shortcut (vj 1, vj 2). A press-only app would lack a thing its two nearest families both have.

**The strongest argument against, and why it does not win.** The match is not exact where it matters most. (1) Among DJs holding is mostly the maker's fixed choice per function, not a switch the user sets per pad; a per-pad or per-clip switch exists on Traktor, Ableton, djay and Resolume only (dj AGAINST; re-check, "medium that it is wanted as a setting"). (2) The group he added himself, DJ / producers, is the one group with no evidence of holding a pad for visuals: those found sequence them (re-check, "low"). (3) No source has a number. (4) A switch costs something: a clip left on hold by accident looks broken (dj AGAINST, a 2011 Ableton user; re-check CONFIRMED), and the NI F1 review says a per-pad mode needs a sensible starting setting (re-check own 6). It does not win because: point 1 argues about where the switch sits, not about whether holding is done; Audio-DNA has no fixed functions that could carry a built-in hold, so the per-item switch of Resolume, Avolites and Onyx is the form that fits (an inference, not a finding). Point 2 is an absence found in press interviews about routing, and the re-check forbids reading it as "they do not". Point 3 cuts both ways. Point 4 is answered by the starting setting: press, so a pad he never touches behaves as he ruled. What the argument against DOES buy: the confidence is medium, not high, and the verdict is "the smallest form", not a family of settings.

**The smallest version.** One switch per mapped key or pad, press or hold, starting on press (that is way b of item 246, as he read it). No velocity, no "Invert" (Resolume's off-while-held), no third "Toggle" style, no global starting setting, no latch-while-holding combination. An even smaller cut exists and is offered as way c: hold for an effect's button and an action only, never for a clip; it avoids the one awkward case (a short hold on a BPM mode clip that ends before the 1) and still covers the use the visuals evidence shows best, the held strobe or flash (vj 4, vj 5).

**Why TEXT below keeps his answer.** He answered "c: none" in round 1 and the box of 246 does not take it back: it orders research and asks "if this is worth doing?". So the assumption stays "no hold setting" and the recommendation is way b.

**Open inside way b (Harmony's picks, not asked; each would cost one question).** Whether the release of a held BPM mode clip clears its layer at once (picked) or at the next 1; whether a release leaves the layer empty (picked, as Resolume's Piano does) or brings back the clip that played before (as a lighting desk's Flash does).
