### THE RACE: what blocks on which side and why the audio thread never does

**Message thread blocks; audio thread never does.** `stopInternal()` (message thread, called from `stop()`) nulls `activeWriter_` (seq_cst), then spin-waits (`while (audioThreadBusy_.load(seq_cst)) std::this_thread::yield();`) until it observes no `push()` call in flight, and only then destroys `threadedWriter_`. This wait is coded UNBOUNDED (no timeout) -- justified because `push()` (the audio thread's only writer of `audioThreadBusy_`) touches nothing that can block: `ScopedBusyFlag`'s constructor/destructor is a single seq_cst atomic store each (wait-free, no CAS loop, no OS call); everything else in `push()`'s call graph is gap-math on stack locals, `memcpy` into fixed-capacity buffers sized once in `prepare()`, and `juce::AudioFormatWriter::ThreadedWriter::write()` itself (JUCE's own documented lock-free SPSC FIFO push, never blocking on disk I/O). The audio thread therefore never waits on anything the message thread does -- it has zero knowledge `stop()` even exists. The message thread's wait is bounded IN PRACTICE by `push()`'s own bounded duration, not by any explicit timeout; I chose this over a bounded/timing-out wait because a timeout that fires while a real writer pointer is still in flight would reopen the exact race by letting `threadedWriter_.reset()` proceed anyway -- a live-show freeze of a fraction of a millisecond (one `push()` call) is the correct trade against a UAF crash on stage. This is a design choice, not the reviewer's literal suggestion -- I judged an unbounded-but-provably-short wait strictly safer than a bounded one whose timeout path reintroduces the bug it exists to prevent.

Confirmed empirically, not just by reasoning: with the wait loop temporarily commented out (mutation test, restored afterward, see below), the new UAF-race test doesn't just fail an assertion -- the process **crashes with SIGABRT** (`libc++abi: terminating`), i.e. real heap corruption from the writer being destroyed while `push()` was still calling into it.

### ASSUMPTIONS

- `push()`'s entire call graph remains RT-safe (no lock/allocation/blocking I/O/unbounded loop) as a standing invariant -- `stopInternal()`'s unbounded busy-wait depends on this remaining true. See RISKS.
- JUCE calls the audio device callback (and thus `push()`) strictly serially from a single thread, never re-entrantly or from two threads at once -- a pre-existing, unchanged assumption of this whole class; it is what makes a plain `bool` (not a counter) correct for `audioThreadBusy_`.
- Exactly two roles are ever concurrent on an `AudioTap` instance (one audio-thread caller of `push()`, one message-thread caller of `stop()`/`prepare()`) -- `seq_cst` on `audioThreadBusy_`/`activeWriter_` is sufficient reasoning for this two-party case without needing a more elaborate hazard-pointer/epoch scheme.
- `ConnectionEngine::evaluate`'s `Envelope(Beats)` path shares the identical `bn` computation as the `Lfo` path I tested (same call site, `ConnectionEngine.cpp` line ~95-96, both switch on the same `c.shape.resetPhaseOnStructural`); I covered `Lfo` explicitly (matching the review's own "every enabled Lfo/Envelope(Beats) connection" framing and the oscillator-level precedent test's scope) since a divergence bug in `bn` itself cannot differ by which source kind consumes it.
