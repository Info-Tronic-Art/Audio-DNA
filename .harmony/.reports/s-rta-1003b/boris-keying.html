Audio-DNA — what works in the Keying, Blend and Transition menus

I put every entry of the Keying menu (13), the layer Blend menu (55) and the Transition menu (55) into the real Audio-DNA app, one at a time, and compared the finished pictures number by number. Nothing in the app was changed. Of the 123 entries, 20 do what their name says, 43 draw exactly the same picture as another entry, 55 do nothing at all, and 5 are wrong in some way. The Keying slider changed not one pixel under any of the 13 keying entries.

Every entry below has a proposal: Keep, Fix or Remove. Nothing is built until you answer, and every Remove is only a proposal.

1. Your questions, answered

You wrote:

Can you figure out how to get everything working in the keying menu? Does the keying slider actually do anything? Maybe we get rid of it. Some elements in the keying menu work and some don’t. Should we use the transparency slider to do the work for keying elements? Also review the compositing menu, right slider in layer strip. These elements don’t work: Creative, 3D,
  1. Does the Keying slider actually do anything? No. I set the Keying value, and the Softness value too, to 0, 0.5 and 1 under each of the 13 keying entries (156 pictures in all) and not one pixel changed. The layer inspector’s Threshold and Softness controls set the same two values. One limit: I set the values in a show file instead of dragging the slider; that the slider writes that value is read from the code, not tried by hand. The likely reason is that no keying entry listens for it (Luma Key listens for it under a different name).
  2. Which keying entries work? Three of 13: Alpha, Chroma Key (removes green only, a fixed green with a hard edge) and Max RGB (dark parts become see-through, bright parts stay solid). Luma Key is wrong: it removes only pure black, nothing else (measured on this Mac). Inverted Luma Key and Saturation Key draw exactly Luma Key’s picture. Luma Is Alpha draws exactly Max RGB’s picture. The other six do nothing (Inverted Luma Is Alpha, Edge Detection, Threshold Mask, Channel Red, Channel Green, Channel Blue): the picture comes out untouched. So the 13 entries make only 4 different pictures.
  3. Should the transparency (opacity) slider do the work for keying? Not as it is today. It works under all 13 entries, but as a fade of the whole layer (at 25% the layer is 25% as strong); it does not move where the key cuts. My suggestion, not a measurement: leave it as the fade, and give the key its own amount (the Keying slider, once it is connected to Luma Key).
  4. What is the right slider in the layer strip, and does it work? I take it to be F, the fade time in seconds for transitions. It works. Set to 2, 4 and 8 seconds, one second into the fade the picture was 50%, 25% and 12.5% of the way across, as it should be. (I could not tell 0 seconds from the half second the app likely uses instead.) Please confirm F is the slider you meant. The menu that goes with it is the problem: only 15 of its 55 entries have a look of their own.
  5. You said Creative and 3D don’t work. Confirmed. In the Blend menu all 9 Creative and all 6 3D entries just draw the plain picture. In the Transition menu all 9 Creative and 5 of the 6 3D entries (all but Flip) are the same soft cross-dissolve. In the Blend menu, everything from “Special” down is really a list of transition names that do nothing there.

2. Entry by entry: what it does today, and my proposal

How I chose. Works: Keep. A duplicate, or an entry that does nothing: Remove, unless the cause (read from the code, not tried) looks like a small, obvious fix: then Fix, marked “likely small fix”. The four blends that ignore opacity and transparency: Fix, but whether that is a defect is your call.

Words used. Works: draws what its name says. Same picture as X: draws exactly what X draws, so it is a duplicate. Does nothing: the picture comes out as the plain one. Wrong: it does something, but not what its name says, or only in some cases.

3. The contact sheets

Click a sheet to see it full size. The small captions on the sheets are the audit’s own words (WORKS, NO-OP = does nothing, ALIAS-OF = same picture as, BROKEN = wrong). One caption is out of date: the keying sheets call Max RGB “same as Luma Is Alpha”, but the ruling is that Max RGB is the original that works and Luma Is Alpha is its copy.

Keying: a green plate over a checkerboard. Look at Chroma Key (the green is gone, the checkerboard shows through) and Luma Key (only the small black block turns into checkerboard); six tiles are the untouched plate.
Keying: strips of ramps over a gradient. Look at Luma Key (one thin line at the far left is all it removes), Chroma Key (cuts the bright end of the green strip and a slice of the rainbow) and Max RGB (every strip fades into the background).
Keying: a picture with its own see-through areas over a checkerboard. Look at Alpha (the soft see-through areas show the checkerboard) against Chroma Key (the picture is solid, and black where it used to be see-through).
Keying controls. For each keying entry: the left three pictures are the Keying slider at 0, 0.5 and 1, the middle three are Softness at 0, 0.5 and 1 (each set of three is identical, so they do nothing), and the right three are opacity at 25%, 50% and 100% (the layer fades).
Blend: one gradient over another. Only five of the 55 tiles differ from the plain picture: Add, Screen, Multiply, Darken and Lighten. Every other tile, Creative and 3D included, looks like Alpha.
Blend: color bars over a grey ramp. The same five tiles differ; the bars take on the grey ramp underneath, darker on the left and lighter on the right. The other 50 look the same as Alpha.
Blend: a picture with its own see-through areas over a gradient. Look at Multiply and Darken (black wherever the picture should be see-through) and at Screen and Lighten (the picture’s color at full strength where it should fade).
Other controls. Chroma Key color and tolerance (they work when set in a show file), opacity under the blend entries (it fades Alpha, Add and Overlay but changes nothing under Screen, Multiply, Darken and Lighten), and at the bottom a layer set to Opaque ignoring every keying and blend entry.
Transitions: a horizontal gradient into a vertical one, three moments of a 6-second fade. Most tiles are the same soft cross-dissolve; look at Cut, the Wipes, Pushes, Zooms, Flip and To Black for the ones with a look of their own.
Transitions: color bars into ramps, the same three moments. The same result with a picture that is easier to follow; Flip and To Black pass through black, and Zoom Out shows the old clip shrinking into the centre.

5. What was not tested

  • Other computers. “Luma Key removes only pure black” and Chroma Key’s hard edge were measured on this Mac only (Apple M1 Pro). The app’s code leaves the exact result open, so another graphics card may draw those four entries (Luma Key, Inverted Luma Key, Saturation Key, Chroma Key) differently.
  • Partly see-through results on a real output. Multiply and Darken with the opacity below full, or with a see-through picture, leave the finished picture partly transparent. Flip and To Black do too in the middle of their fades. I measured the picture the app draws, not what the preview panel, an Output window, Syphon or a recording shows for it.
  • Nothing on screen was clicked or dragged. The values were set through show files and the app’s control interface. That the Keying slider writes the value I changed, that the layer strip’s menu re-shows the right entry, and that no on-screen control sets Chroma Key’s color or tolerance or a layer’s Opaque/Transparent setting are read from the code, not tried by hand.
  • The layer inspector’s three menus (Blend Mode, Keying, Transition) were matched to the same entries by reading the code. Its Blend Mode list likely has only the first 25 of the 55 blends and likely calls the first two “Normal” and “Additive” where the strip says “Alpha” and “Add”. I did not open them.
  • Left out entirely: clip opacity before keying; keying on a video or a generated source; transitions between pictures with see-through areas; a transition on a keyed layer.
  • The F slider at 0 seconds. I could not tell an instant change from the half second the app likely substitutes. It was checked at 2, 4 and 8 seconds.
  • Saved shows. What a saved show that already uses an entry proposed for removal should do was not looked at; I would bring that question with the plan.
