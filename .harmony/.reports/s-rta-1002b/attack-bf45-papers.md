### SEAT SIGNAL / AUDIO (bf45 attack) — VERDICT AMEND
A1 [MUST] Plan's Once mode is nearly unusable for Boris. It pins the start at the next bar line on the FIRST evaluation, and SignalRegistry::evaluateAll runs every envelope every 120 Hz tick whether or not any control uses it. A Once envelope therefore fires at app start or load and sits at y(1) before any control connects. Restart is only a panel button or a test REST route, since Q4 (pad/key) is deferred. The performer cannot trigger it.
  EVIDENCE: plan A3 'pinned ... on the first evaluation, on requestRestart(), or on a backward clock jump'; plan Q4 default 'not now'; plan F9 evaluateAll is per-tick for all signals (MainComponent.cpp:4030-4039, as the plan cites). I read the current code, EnvelopeSignal.h:60-69: Once is a no-op today, so there is no usage precedent to lean on.
  FIX: Do not start Once on first evaluation. Arm it idle at y(0) until requestRestart() or a connection bind. Better, start it on the first tick after a control binds, or on a clip/trigger edge. Either ship a restart binding in this lane or label Once 'test only' in the UI. Add a test where Once is evaluated for 100 ticks with no restart and must stay at y(0).
A2 [MUST] The Once/Resync design infers a Resync from a 'backward clock jump of more than 1 beat' in the hybrid clock beatPhase + beatInBar + 4*barsSinceResync. This is the beat-wrap family that Pitfalls 42 and 48 warn about. A hard realign or Tap inside a beat moves the clock by less than 1 beat, so it is not detected and the pinned start goes stale. A forward Tap/realign makes Once skip. A structural reset (resetPhaseOnStructural_, which switches to barCount) jumps the clock backward and silently re-pins a running Once. A message-thread stall or tempo-command lag (Pitfall 48) can show up as a jump.
  EVIDENCE: EnvelopeSignal.h:55-63 (barsElapsed switches between barCount and barsSinceResync); pitfalls.md:93 (Pitfall 42, clocks integrate totalBeatCount + beatPhase and never the wrap); plan A3 mirrors ConnectionEngine.cpp:59-62 heuristics without citing the tests behind them.
  FIX: Pin Once on a monotone counter (totalBeatCount + beatPhase, raw < lastRaw absorbed as continuity, as RecorderClock does). Take restart from an explicit event, not a heuristic. Add tests for a Tap realign within a beat, a forward realign, a structural-reset toggle, and a 2.3-beat stall.
A3 [MUST] Test C1-1 (two tones, mids must be 'silent') is fragile as written. A hard-switched 60 Hz to 12 kHz tone at an arbitrary phase has a discontinuity whose splatter lands in the 250-4000 Hz band. The -30 dB relative-to-strongest silence rule may or may not trip, so the test can be flaky or can fail for the wrong reason. The fixture 'gen-envelope-wav.py' and the C1 test do not mention a fade at the joint.
  EVIDENCE: plan C1 test 1 (1 s 60 Hz then 1 s 12 kHz) and the silent rule at 10^(-30/20) and 1e-3 (plan C1 DSP bullet). INFERRED, not measured. The plan's own scratch check (F25) covers only the bend math and RDP, not the band leakage.
  FIX: Fade the tones in and out (about 10 ms Hann) or switch at a zero crossing. Measure the actual mids/lows ratio in the scratch run and put the threshold margin in the plan. Add a case where the relative threshold should NOT trip: a sample with real mids at -31 dB must still return a usable band.
A4 [SHOULD] The silent-band rule is relative (-30 dB under the strongest band) and the per-band peak normalisation is peak-based. A bass-heavy loop with audible mids 31 dB down is reported 'No mids in this sample' and disabled. One loud transient (a pop or click) normalises the whole band to a small value, which flattens the envelope.
  EVIDENCE: plan C1 'Normalise each band to its own peak. A band whose peak is below the strongest band x 10^(-30/20), or below 1e-3, is silent'. The live analysis uses a slowly decaying running max (SpectralFeatures.cpp:166-193 per plan F20), not peak normalisation.
  FIX: Normalise to a high percentile (e.g. 99th) of the follower output, not the single peak. Soften the silence rule to an absolute floor in dBFS plus a user override. Say how this scaling relates to the live band scaling, because users will tune connections against the live signals.
A5 [SHOULD] Decimation is under-specified. The plan samples band RMS 'every hop = max(1 ms, length/4096)' and also runs a peak-hold release follower with tau 60-120 ms. For a 10-minute sample the hop is about 146 ms, longer than the 10-40 ms RMS window and longer than the follower tau. If the follower or RMS runs at hop rate, transients between hops are lost and the release ballistics are wrong. The ghost then max-pools the decimated points, so the loss is already baked in.
  EVIDENCE: plan C1 DSP bullets (window 40/20/10/20 ms, hop = max(1 ms, length/4096), tau 120/80/60/80 ms) and Q5 default 'whole sample up to 10 minutes'. No test uses a sample over a few seconds.
  FIX: Run the RMS and follower at the audio rate, or at a fixed 1-2 ms hop. Max-pool into the 1024 ghost from that fine envelope. Add a test with a 60 s synthetic sample of sparse clicks: every click must survive.
A6 [SHOULD] Filters are causal with no latency compensation. The LR4 pairs, the 20 Hz high-pass, the RMS window and the follower all delay the envelope against the audio onset by several ms to tens of ms. Boris wants the envelope to 'emulate the audio's behavior', and the lows are delayed most. The plan accounts for the m4a priming (R10) but not for its own filter delay. The C1-2 tolerance of +-0.02 of 2 s (40 ms) hides it. The 20 Hz high-pass is also given a step input at sample start with no settling.
  EVIDENCE: plan C1 DSP and test 2 ('x within +-0.02'); R10 only names Core Audio priming.
  FIX: Compensate the group delay (centre the RMS window, or shift the ghost by the measured delay). Tighten test 2 to +-1 ghost point. Pre-roll or zero-state the 20 Hz high-pass. State the lows-versus-highs offset as a test.
A7 [SHOULD] Low-rate and odd-rate inputs are untested. A 4 kHz crossover at 8 or 11.025 kHz input sits at or above Nyquist. juce::dsp LinkwitzRiley asserts or misbehaves when cutoff >= sampleRate/2, and the plan has no guard. Zero-length, NaN/Inf-containing (corrupt decode) and DC-only input are not covered either. Pure silence is only covered via the 1e-3 floor, with no test for an all-zero file, where division by a zero peak is the risk.
  EVIDENCE: plan C1 test list 1-8 (no sample-rate or silence/NaN case); F19 cites LinkwitzRileyFilter processSample but no rate precondition.
  FIX: Clamp or disable the highs band when 4 kHz >= 0.45 * sampleRate and say so in the notice. Add tests for an 8 kHz source, an all-zero file, a DC offset, and a NaN sample (sanitize to 0).
A8 [SHOULD] The bend-curve claim is wrong beyond the midpoint. The plan says the old Exponential (t*t) 'equals bend +0.282', and A7 maps the legacy curve combo to that bend. Only f(0.5) matches (0.25). At t=0.25, t*t = 0.0625 while f = (e^(0.549)-1)/(e^(2.19)-1) = 0.092, so the shape changes for any existing envelope on Exponential. The plan also calls the Mod 2 behaviour change 'none'.
  EVIDENCE: plan F25 and A7 versus EnvelopeSignal.h:90-101 (Exponential is t*t). I recomputed f by hand from the plan's own formula in F-4. INFERRED arithmetic, not run.
  FIX: Either keep a true quadratic option for the legacy mapping (a CurveType preserved in the definition) or state in the docs that the legacy Exponential becomes an approximation. Add a regression test comparing Mod 2 with Exponential before and after, at several t.
A9 [SHOULD] 'Stretched to the set beat/bar length' is implemented as stretching the whole sample onto the envelope length, with no relation between the sample's own tempo/length and the set BPM. A 1.9 s kick loop on a 4-bar envelope at 120 BPM (8 s) is slowed 4.2x, so the follower tau and the sample's attack/decay timing are stretched too. Nothing warns Boris, and there is no beat-aligned import. The test and probe only check durationSec, not that the stretched shape matches the intended rhythm.
  EVIDENCE: plan F-6, F-5, Q5 default; plan G3 e6. Boris BF5 answer 'all defaults good', backlog line 46-48 asks about stretching to the set beat/bar length.
  FIX: At load, report the sample length against the envelope length as a ratio ('stretch 4.2x'). Offer to set the envelope length from the sample's detected bar count, or at least show the bpm-implied length. Apply tau in stretched time, or document that it is not.
A10 [NIT] Boris's 'extract the envelope live' is read as offline analysis (F-6c rejects live). That is defensible. But the plan has no fallback for 'live' capture, and the rejection reasons are partly wrong: a stretch to bar length is possible from a recorded take of the live band signals.
  EVIDENCE: plan F-6(c); backlog lines 17-18.
  FIX: State the reading explicitly to Boris with Q-answer wording ('analysed from the file, not played'), so he can correct it.
A11 [NIT] Sacred Rule 3 and the analysis thread are untouched and the claim is true (loader runs on a 1-thread low-priority pool, juce_dsp off the analysis thread). Nothing prevents the loader pool from competing for cores with analysis at the default thread priority on small machines. No budget test is given for the loader while the analysis thread runs.
  EVIDENCE: plan C2 (1-thread low-priority pool 'EnvSample') and G2 (TSan only); no analysis-hop timing gate while a 10-minute decode runs.
  FIX: Add a probe row: analyse a 10-minute file during live audio and assert analysis-hop overruns stay at the baseline.
